Five rules that matter more than the exercises

  • Log every session — exercise, weight, reps. You cannot progress what you do not record.
  • Add reps before weight. Hit the top of the range on all sets, then add load and drop back down.
  • Stop 2 reps short of failure in month one. On a six-day split, recovery is the limiting factor, not effort.
  • Never skip a session to do a longer one later. Six consistent 20-minute sessions beat two heroic hours.
  • Take Thursday off properly. The rest day is what makes six days work. Morning block and walking, nothing else.
Eat more to pay for the extra sessions

Doubling from three sessions to six adds roughly 900–1,200 kcal of expenditure a week. On a surplus of about 500 a day, that is a fifth of it gone — you would still gain, but slower than the 0.5 kg a week the plan is built around.

Add 150–200 kcal a day. Either works:

  • A third chilla at dinner — 30 g more besan, about 130 kcal and 7 g protein.
  • Or 20 g more oats in the jar plus 5 extra almonds, about 110 kcal.

That puts you near 3,000 kcal a day. Watch the scale rather than the arithmetic — if the weekly average is not rising 0.35 kg, add another 200.

On six days being manageable

You have trained harder than this before, so capability is not the question. Two things are different this time, and both are about the budget rather than the effort.

First, you are eating for weight gain now, so every session you add has to be paid for in food. Second, you are starting from zero conditioning — whatever you did before, your body is not currently adapted to it, and week one of six consecutive days will produce real soreness. Two sets on Monday, and expect Wednesday to feel worse than Tuesday.

If by week two you are dreading sessions or sleeping badly, drop Sunday's pull and go to five days. That is a sensible adjustment, not a failure.

Method

  2. Saturday morning — drain completely. Rinse once more, drain again, and rest the jar at an angle so it keeps draining.

Drain properly every single time. Standing water at the bottom of the jar is the one thing that makes sprouts smell and spoil. Tilt the jar, don't just tip it.
Kala chana sprouts slower than moong and will still be firm with a short tail when the moong is ready. That is normal and it is eaten that way.
If you want to be careful: sprouting is warm and moist, which also suits bacteria. Steaming 3–4 minutes removes almost all of that risk and makes them easier on the stomach, at the cost of a little vitamin C. Plenty of people eat them raw without trouble — your call.
Two batches a week, and the second is a half batch. You have three office days but a full batch only makes two servings — so the second run of the week is half the size. Do not make one big weekly batch instead: sprouts keep about 3 days refrigerated, and Friday's serving would be five days old.

Soak in the fridge, not on the counter. Figs, dates and raisins are sugar-heavy, and nine hours in water at Bangalore room temperature can start to ferment. The fridge removes that entirely.
Why soak the almonds. The brown skin holds tannins and most of the phytic acid, which binds minerals like zinc and iron and reduces how much you absorb. Your jar and shake are already high in phytate from oats, seeds and chia, so cutting some of it is worth the two minutes. Peeling is optional — the skin also carries useful polyphenols, so it is close to a wash either way.
Cashews stay dry, correctly. They are soft enough to blend as they are, and soaking turns them slimy.
Keep the fenugreek separate. Its soaking water is bitter and gets thrown away. The dry-fruit water does not — drain it off, but there is no harm in a splash going into the blender.
Whey goes in last, briefly. Long blending whips air into it and you end up drinking foam, which feels far more filling than it should.

Made fresh, in the same twenty minutes as the chilla. The rinsing is the whole point — done properly, soya causes almost no gas and loses the beany taste completely.

Method
  1. 8:05 PM — boil enough water to cover the chunks generously. Take it off the heat, add the dry chunks, and leave them.
  2. Make the chilla batter while they soak. The batter needs a 10-minute rest anyway, so the two run in parallel.
  3. 8:18 PM — the chunks should be fully swollen and soft through. Drain.
  4. Rinse under cold running water. This cools them enough to handle straight away.
  5. Squeeze each handful firmly to push the water out. Rinse and squeeze again — twice in total.
  6. Straight into the tomato-onion base with turmeric, jeera and 1 tsp groundnut oil.
Why it matters: the soaking water carries the beany flavour and much of what causes bloating. Skipping the squeeze-and-rinse is why most people say soya disagrees with them.
Yield: 40 g dry becomes roughly 120 g soaked.
Changed from the printed sheets. The PDFs say to soak soya the night before and refrigerate it. Make it fresh instead. Soya chunks are already cooked during manufacture, so rehydrating is just adding water back — there is nothing to gain from a longer soak, and soaked soya sitting in the fridge is a moist, protein-rich food you would rather not store. The soak is passive and overlaps with the batter rest, so fresh costs about five minutes.

Method

Ramp the fibre up, do not jump

Chia, fenugreek, figs, makhana, oats, seeds, besan and isabgol together is a large increase from where you are now. Full quantities from day one will cause bloating and gas, and that is the most common reason people abandon a plan.

Week 1 — half chia (5 g), no fenugreek in either meal, isabgol every other night.
Week 2 — full chia, 1 g fenugreek, isabgol nightly.
Week 3 onward — full quantities. Keep water at 3.2 litres or the extra fibre works against you.
